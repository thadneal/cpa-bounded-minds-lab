# CPA Bounded Minds Laboratory closeout

Version **1.0.0**

Status: **experimental program complete**

The Bounded Minds Laboratory is closed after nine protocols, three generations of validation/stress methodology, fresh targeted holdouts for the final two mechanisms, and controlled operating-envelope work. No Protocol 10 is planned.

The laboratory asked:

> What develops among bounded minds when they can exchange selected public evidence without collapsing their independent interiors?

The answer is not one social algorithm. The lab produced a set of behavioral pressures, failure modes, and methodological constraints that survived different forms of pressure well enough to carry into later CPA work.

## What the laboratory established

### 1. Foreign evidence can be useful without becoming lived authority

Protocol 01 showed that compact second-hand traces can provide a useful prior while remaining weaker than direct receiver consequence. Its seed family predates the later lived-circumstance replication policy, so this is best treated as a mechanism demonstration rather than strong longitudinal confirmation.

### 2. Preserved private difference can have corrective value

Protocol 02 showed that complementary private error structures can outperform premature synchronization and still converge under common consequence. Its replication semantics share the same early-lab limitation as P01.

### 3. The path by which a belief was earned can matter

Protocol 03 showed a real difference between developmental transfer and doctrinal transfer. Later holdout, challenge, and causal surfaces qualified the result: developmental instability is information about uncertainty, but the frozen scalar penalty for instability is not durable architecture.

### 4. Public communication should preserve epistemic shape

Protocol 04 produced one of the cleaner findings in the lab. Typed public posture survived frozen validation unusually well. Controlled equal-budget comparison later showed that no single reducer dominates all warrant distributions. The durable result is preservation of source-specific epistemic shape, not one aggregation rule.

### 5. Repeated coordination can compress into revisable culture

Protocol 05 demonstrated distributed convention without a central culture owner. Stress work exposed the opposing demands of retention and revisability. A stale convention can remain successful enough to reinforce itself, creating cultural hysteresis before obvious failure.

### 6. Agreement and evidential independence are different

Protocol 06 showed why echoed reports should not count as independent corroboration. The principle survived, while the frozen signature-grouping heuristic failed controlled tests by merging genuinely independent roots under some conditions. Ancestry should remain uncertain dependence evidence with both positive and negative provenance cues.

### 7. Standing can transfer opportunity without transferring authority

Protocol 07 showed that second-hand standing can buy useful early opportunity when a recommender generalizes, while imposing real cost when it does not. Recommender credibility and expected local generalizability are different variables. Eventual revocation does not erase developmental cost paid before correction.

### 8. Strategic public influence can remain answerable

Protocol 08 moved from passive imperfect evidence to a peer that adapts its public presentation for influence. Its fresh twenty-seed holdout returned 20/20 Support, and controlled surfaces exposed clear boundaries. Receiver-owned, consequence-grounded permission preserved useful influence without private-state inspection, but delayed/noisy consequence, pervasive divergence, and weak or late betrayal made the limits visible.

### 9. Permission itself has ancestry

Protocol 09 closed the social arc. Its fresh holdout returned 20/20 Support and 200/200 checks. Recursive endorsement amplified weak circular standing by about `7.53x` without new independent grounding. Authority ancestry limited that apparent legitimacy while preserving independently grounded social opportunity.

Controlled surfaces then showed that root diversity matters more than simple network closure, lineage fidelity must rise with circulation depth, circularity is not evidence of falsehood, and sufficiently noisy receiver consequence can turn caution into over-deterrence.

See `PROTOCOL_09_VALIDATION_RESULTS.md` for the complete final evidence record.

## Cross-protocol synthesis

Several themes recur strongly enough to leave the lab:

1. **Private interiors should remain private by default.** Useful social cognition did not require direct access to another mind's hidden state.
2. **Public surfaces need epistemic type.** Estimate, standing, uncertainty, origin/dependence, and consequence quality should not collapse too early into one confidence value.
3. **Permission is local and revisable.** Another mind may earn influence, but durable authority remains answerable to the receiver's own consequence.
4. **Dependence matters at several layers.** Evidence has ancestry, recommendations have developmental context, and authority itself can recirculate.
5. **Correction has latency and cost.** A mechanism that eventually recovers can still be developmentally bad if it spends too long confidently wrong.
6. **Communication cost is part of cognition.** Useful social breadth must justify its work rather than being treated as free context.
7. **Social openness has an opportunity cost.** More peers, more communication, and more revisability are not automatically better.
8. **Channel uncertainty differs from source uncertainty.** Bad feedback can make a good peer look bad. The architecture needs to preserve that distinction.
9. **Topology is secondary to causal and epistemic structure.** Cycles become dangerous when they manufacture apparent independence, not merely because a graph contains a loop.
10. **Behavioral constraints deserve inheritance before formulas do.** The lab repeatedly found useful principles while falsifying or qualifying the particular scalar rules used to expose them.

## What the laboratory did not establish

The closeout intentionally leaves several claims unmade:

- that the experiment-local standing equations should appear in CPA;
- that explicit root IDs or path bit sketches are the correct ancestry representation;
- that a central reputation, culture, or provenance service should exist;
- that one public aggregation function should govern every coalition;
- that bounded peers in these synthetic worlds are adequate models of human social cognition;
- that social evidence can replace direct contact with consequence;
- that an observer can reliably identify another mind when the source is not already given.

That final omission defines the successor laboratory.

## Methodological result

The lab's experimental method changed substantially during its life:

```text
mechanism discovery
  -> varied lived histories
  -> frozen unseen holdout
  -> adversarial generator search
  -> controlled causal surfaces
  -> targeted fresh holdout for later mechanisms
  -> explicit operating-envelope falsification
```

This progression should be inherited. A perfect development matrix is not enough. Holdouts should be frozen before observation, challenge sets should not masquerade as confirmation, and controlled probes should be allowed to make mechanisms lose in interpretable ways.

## Frozen state

Protocols 01-09 are closed experimental records. Their frozen mechanism/world source hashes remain in `FROZEN_PROTOCOL_SHA256.txt` and are verified before normal build/self-test by `scripts/verify.ps1` and `scripts/verify.sh`.

Version 1.0.0 adds no Protocol 10 and makes no change to any SHA-256-frozen protocol/world file. Future work that changes those mechanisms is a new experiment or a new implementation program and should use new evidence.

## Handoff

The next laboratory changes the unit of uncertainty.

Bounded Minds began with peers whose existence and communication channels were already given. The **Trace and Interface Laboratory** should ask whether a bounded observer can infer structured causal influence when the source itself is hidden behind a limited interface.

That shift is large enough to deserve a clean new laboratory rather than Protocol 10.

See `NEXT_LAB.md` for the finalized handoff.
